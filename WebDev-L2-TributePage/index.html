ABOUT HIM

Sir Chandrasekhara Venkata Raman

Sir Chandrasekhara Venkata Raman was one of India's most celebrated scientists. He was born on 7 November 1888 in Tiruchirappalli, India. From an early age, he showed a strong interest in science and mathematics and developed a passion for understanding the natural world.

Raman pursued his education with great dedication and later worked in the Indian Finance Department while continuing his scientific research. His curiosity about light, sound and physical phenomena encouraged him to spend much of his free time conducting experiments and studying scientific problems.

His most famous scientific contribution was the discovery known as the Raman Effect. Through his experiments, Raman demonstrated that when light passes through a transparent material, a small portion of the light changes its wavelength. This discovery became an important development in the study of molecular physics.

In 1930, C. V. Raman received the Nobel Prize in Physics for his work on the scattering of light and the discovery of the Raman Effect. His achievements inspired generations of Indian students and scientists and established him as one of the leading scientists of his time.

HIS GREATEST DISCOVERY

The Raman Effect

🔬

What Did He Discover?

C. V. Raman discovered that light can interact with molecules in a transparent material and that a small part of the scattered light can have a different wavelength from the original light.

💡

Why Is It Important?

The Raman Effect became an important tool for studying the structure and properties of materials and molecules. It continues to have applications in scientific research and analytical techniques.
